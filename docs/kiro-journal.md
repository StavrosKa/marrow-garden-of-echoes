## 2026-11-16 - marrow-input task 2: frame types, landmark validity and geometry

- Kiro feature used: spec, task 2 of `marrow-input` (model: Claude Opus 5.5)
- What was built:
  - Task 1 was reviewed, approved and committed as c13d2ab "Add tuning module and validation". This entry records that; the task 1 entry is unchanged.

A landmark is valid if and only if visibility >= `tuning.minVisibility` and 0 <= x <= 1 and 0 <= y <= 1. Every comparison with NaN is false, so NaN is invalid without a separate check.
  - `packages/marrow/src/core/geometry.ts`, internal and not on the barrel: `dist`, `clamp`, `add`, `sub`, `scale` and `lerp`, on the `Vec2` imported from `frame.ts`. `lerp` is a + (b - a) * t, the form of the smoothing step in Req 9.4. `dist` takes the square root of the sum of squares, so exact inputs such as 3-4-5 give exact results.
  - `marrow/core` now also exports `Vec2`, `WristId`, `Landmark`, `PoseLandmarks`, `PoseFrame`, `LANDMARK_INDEX` and `isLandmarkValid`.

Req 17.16 says time is "never counted for two pointers", which is about pointers, not targets. Placement keeps the targets of a round apart (Req 14.10), so the playground never shows this. This is the question for the user in the final report.
  2. The step in which ownership is released. Nobody acquires in it, neither another pointer nor the same one: a target is open to acquisition in a step only if, at the start of that step, it had no owner, no latch, and its activation had not produced a selection. This is Req 17.10 ("from the next step on") for the Outside release and the Invalid cancel, and for a selection the target is also latched and its activation used. I applied the same rule to the two other cases the text leaves open, so there is one rule to state and test: a changed activation id under an owner (Req 17.14) drops the hold and acquisition follows from the next step; the step in which the last latched pointer leaves ends the latch, and a pointer that was not latched acquires from the next step. A target with no owner at the start of the step whose activation id changes is acquired in that very step, like a target seen for the first time.
  3. Boundaries, confirmed against the text. "Exceeds 150 ms" (Req 17.7) and "exceeds 300 ms" (Req 17.8) are strict: a stint of exactly the window keeps the hold, one more millisecond ends it in that step. "Reaches 1500 ms" (Req 17.11) is `>=`. Inside is distance `<=` radius (Req 17.3), tested with the centre at the origin, where the position (radius, 0) is at distance sqrt(radius * radius) = radius exactly, and one ULP beyond it is Outside.
  4. The latch. A wrist that is not in `pointers` at all is an invalid pointer: as owner it is Invalid (cancel after more than 300 ms), and in a latch it does not leave, because Req 17.12 lets a pointer leave only in a step in which it is valid and outside the hit area. If a wrist appears twice in `pointers` the first entry counts. A target that is not supplied loses its hold and keeps its latch and its used activations (Req 17.14); no pointer can leave a latch in a step in which the target has no hit area, since leaving is judged against this step's hit area. The latch also survives a change of activation id, so a pointer that has stayed inside cannot take the next activation until it has gone valid and outside once.
  5. A radius or centre that changes under the same activation id: the hold continues, and each step classifies with that step's hit area (tested: a shrinking radius turns the owner Outside for a step and the same hold goes on when the centre follows the pointer).
  6. dt. Used as given, not clamped a second time and not checked: a dt above `maxClockStepMs` counts in full (a test passes 3 steps' worth and requires it), and a negative or NaN dt is outside the contract (Req 16.2: the Clocks return 0..`maxClockStepMs`) and gets no special handling, so it would subtract from or poison the timers. A dt of 0 changes no timer and no progress, but still classifies and still resets the other timer.

- Decisions on the points of the job:
  1. Boundaries, confirmed against the text. "Exceeds" is strict for lost time: the hold begins when lost time is greater than `trackingHoldAfterMs` (Req 18.3) and the overlay shows when it is greater than `trackingOverlayAfterMs` (Req 18.4). "Reaches" is `>=` for valid time: the hold ends when valid time is at least `trackingResumeMs` (Req 18.6). The one-wrist hint needs "more than" `oneWristHintMs` (Req 18.9), so it is strict too. Each is tested at the threshold (no change), one millisecond over (the change, in that step) and in full steps.
  2. Lost time during a hold. It keeps counting through the hold, which the overlay rule needs ("WHEN continuous lost time exceeds 1000 ms during a tracking hold"), and the start of a hold does not reset it: the hold begins at just over 300 ms and the overlay at just over 1000 ms of lost time, 700 ms later, not 1000 ms after the hold. It is reset in any step in which an active wrist is valid (Req 18.2), so it is already 0 in the step in which a hold ends, because a hold ends only in a step with a valid wrist; nothing else resets it. Once shown, the overlay stays until the hold ends, through a valid stretch that resets lost time (Req 18.4).
  3. Lost time during calibration. It counts only from the end of calibration. While calibration runs, or in simulated input, the whole state is reset in every step (no timer runs), so no hold can be due in the first step of play. Reason: Req 11.17 and 18.10 suspend the lifecycle during calibration, the calm hold does not need the wrists (Req 12.5), so a player whose hands rest in the lap at the end of calibration would otherwise start play inside a hold with the overlay due in a second. With this rule the player has `trackingHoldAfterMs` of grace, which is what Req 18.3 gives anyone outside calibration. A hold that is running when calibration starts (for example after Recalibrate) ends in that step, with the events of every flag that was on.
  4. The one-wrist hint. With one active wrist it can never show: it needs another active wrist that is valid (and with two entries for one wrist in the configuration, the wrists are counted as a set). The invalid time is per wrist and counts the presence time since that wrist's last valid step, also while both wrists were invalid; so after a hold ends, a wrist that never came back can show the hint in that very step. A wrist that becomes invalid during calibration and stays invalid is timed from the end of calibration, as in point 3: the hint shows after more than 1000 ms of play. The hint does not show during a hold (the overlay is on). Once shown, it is hidden when its wrist is valid again or a hold begins, and only then (Req 18.9 names those two): if the other wrist goes invalid too, the hint stays for up to `trackingHoldAfterMs` until the hold begins, and does not blink if the other wrist comes back. That needs the state to remember which wrist the hint is about (`hintWrist`). It does not show when the other wrist is invalid in the same step, before a hold begins.

A valid step still resets lost time and the invalid time of that wrist even at delta 0, because lost time is the time since the last valid step and a valid step is a valid step whatever the clock does. Tested at each threshold with many steps of delta 0 and with every combination of validity.

  2. A change that is pending when another round ends is queued: the later change goes to the end of a queue of axes. Merging it into the pending one and dropping it were the other readings. Reason: Req 19.4 gives each round that ends with enough difficulties its own raise, and Req 19.8 only delays a raise, it does not merge or cancel one. Req 19.11 allows one change per event, so two cannot be applied together, and one per event, in order, is what the queue does. Each decision is made against the levels and the last changed axis as they will be once the queue is applied, so every queued change is valid when its turn comes (a change above the top level is never queued, and the fallback of Req 19.5 sees the same levels), and the queue never holds more changes than the two axes can still rise, which keeps a session within 2 * `helpMaxLevel` changes (Req 19.13).   3. "Continue with help" (Req 19.9) is accepted in every state, with any flags, and it is never a difficulty. In a round with a current step (the first one not completed) it emits `revealStep` for that step and counts it completed. Reason: Req 19.9 says "accept"; with nothing left to reveal, an output would name a step that does not exist, and a refusal would break "accepts in every state".
